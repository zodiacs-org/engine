# The L1 position gap against Horizons (ZOD-206), 13 November 2026

The conformance suite's 240 L1 vectors (24 TT instants from 1851 to 2148, ten
bodies) hold NASA JPL Horizons's apparent geocentric ecliptic places from
DE441, carried into the IAU 2006/2000A true ecliptic of date
(`conformance/arbiters/l1/`). `@zodiacs/engine` 1.1.0-rc.3 passed 48 of them
at the suite's tolerance of 1″. This folder records the starting score, what
was done about it, and every case scored against each published limit.

## In short

| | default source (astronomy-engine 2.1.19) | DE440 pack through `withEphemeris` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| suite tolerance, 1″ in longitude and in latitude | 48 of 240 | **240 of 240** |
| 30″ in longitude (longitude only) | 240 of 240 | 240 of 240 |
| receipt bound, whole angle (`src/calc-bounds.ts`, `geocentric/apparent`) | 237 of 240 | 240 of 240 (not this source's bound, see below) |
| largest longitude difference | 18.775″ (Neptune, L1-POS-0209) | 0.0127″ (Moon, L1-POS-0002) |
| largest latitude difference | 19.029″ (Saturn, L1-POS-0237) | 0.0011″ (Moon, L1-POS-0222) |
| largest whole-angle difference | 19.039″ (Saturn, L1-POS-0237) | 0.0127″ (Moon, L1-POS-0002) |

Figures are rounded up. Engine: 1.1.0-rc.3 built from `origin/main` at
`8da3a4e` (no file under `src/` is changed by this work). Node 22.22.2 for the
committed results. `results/summary.json` has the unrounded figures by body
and `results/cases.md` the 240 cases, one row each, for both sources.

The gap closes when the engine's positions come from DE440. The engine as
installed is unchanged: it still computes positions with astronomy-engine and
still passes 48 of 240 at 1″. Nothing in the package was edited, because a
packed file changes only with a new candidate, and no pack is shipped.

## What was changed

1. `conformance/adapters/zodiacs-engine.mjs` takes two environment variables,
   `ZODIACS_ENGINE_DE440_PACK` and `ZODIACS_ENGINE_DE440_SHA256`. With both
   set it opens the pack with `@zodiacs/engine/de440` (which refuses the file
   unless its SHA-256 is the one given) and answers the position requests
   with `natalChart` inside the root's `withEphemeris`. Without them it does
   exactly what it did, so CI's check of `results/zodiacs-engine.json` is
   untouched. It uses the engine's public entry points only.
2. `conformance/results/zodiacs-engine-de440.json` is that run on L1, written
   by the harness, with its notes beside it. `RESULTS.md` and
   `results/summary.json` are regenerated by `report.mjs`, whose run order
   gained one entry so that the new section follows the engine's own.
3. `conformance/README.md` shows the command, and the engine's own notes
   replace "A precise backend is planned to close this gap" with a pointer to
   the new section.
4. This folder.

No vector, arbiter, tolerance or bound was changed.

## Which limits, and why (A2)

- **The suite's tolerance: 1″ in longitude (on the circle) and 1″ in
  latitude.** Each vector carries it. This is the limit the 48 of 240 was
  scored against, and the only one of the three that tests a precise source.
- **30″ in longitude.** The public bound, as ZOD-97 (job 1) read it: a bound
  on ecliptic longitude only. It is scored here on `|Δlon|` alone. The
  whole-angle difference is reported in its own column and is not held to
  30″.
- **The receipt bounds of `src/calc-bounds.ts`**, rows `geocentric/apparent`,
  first figure: the largest whole-angle difference from Horizons (DE441) the
  calc corpus saw for each body over 32 instants
  (`docs/evidence/calc-api/rc16`). They were measured on the default source.
  `calc()` refuses to run inside `withEphemeris`, so no receipt carries these
  bounds for a DE440 chart; scoring the DE440 run against them shows only
  that it is far inside them.

**ΔT does not enter.** Job 1 found that Horizons holds a fixed future ΔT, so
a future UTC instant is not a fair comparison with it. Here the Horizons
requests were made with `TIME_TYPE TT` (see any file in
`conformance/sources/horizons-24/`) and the engine is called with
`timeScale: "tt"`, so both sides read the same TT instant and no ΔT model is
used by either. Seven of the 24 instants (70 cases) fall after the Horizons
fetch date of 2026-09-22; they are scored like the rest for that reason.

### The DE440 run

All 240 cases are within 1″, and so within 30″ in longitude. What is left is
at the level of the arbiter itself: its own check against DE440s
(`conformance/arbiters/l1/README.md`) finds a rotation shared by all bodies
of at most 2.82 mas from the frame model, and a lunar difference between
DE440 and DE441 of up to 12.63 mas at 1851. The Moon's 0.0127″ here is that
case (L1-POS-0002, 1851). The nine other bodies reach 0.0038″; what part
of that lies beyond the arbiter's 2.82 mas was not attributed here.

### The default source, and three cases over their receipt bound

On the default source every case is within 30″ in longitude (largest
18.775″), and 237 of 240 are within the body's receipt bound. Three are not:

| id | body | TT | Δlon | Δlat | angle | receipt bound |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| L1-POS-0117 | Saturn | 1986-11-13 13:47:55 | 4.781 | 17.599 | 18.237 | 18 |
| L1-POS-0164 | Venus | 2026-09-22 17:01:08 | −12.595 | 3.550 | 13.015 | 11 |
| L1-POS-0237 | Saturn | 2148-12-30 07:33:00 | 0.627 | 19.029 | 19.039 | 18 |

These are not new failures of the engine; its output is what it was. They
say that the receipt bound, a largest difference seen at 32 instants, is not
a bound at these other instants. The broad sample on `main`
(`docs/evidence/broad-sample-2026-11-13/`) and the DE440 sweep of ZOD-65
(draft PR 92) found the same for Venus and Saturn on far more instants.
Replacing those bounds is ZOD-65's work and was left alone here. One caveat:
the receipt bounds were measured in Horizons's own frames and these vectors
are in the IAU 2006/2000A frame of date. The angle between two directions
does not depend on the frame it is written in, but the calc corpus's own
comparison was not rerun here.

So "all 240 within the published limits" holds as follows: with the DE440
source, on every limit; with the default source, on the 30″ longitude bound
only.

## What this does not do

- It does not change what an installed engine computes. A caller gets these
  positions only by compiling a pack from their own copy of the kernel and
  running inside `withEphemeris` (`docs/de440.md`).
- CI cannot rerun the DE440 section, since no pack or kernel is in the
  repository. CI's tier-B job has the kernel but not the compiler. The
  section is a committed measurement, like the Swiss Ephemeris one.
- 240 cases at 24 instants are a sample. The claim is about these vectors.
- The true node and the speeds are not in L1 and were not compared.

## Rerun

`tools/rerun.sh` fetches the kernel, checks its digest, compiles and seals
the pack, checks its digest, runs both conformance checks and the scorer, and
compares the scorer's output with `results/` byte for byte (Node 22.22.2).
`tools/score.mjs` alone reruns the 240 cases through both sources in a few
seconds, given a pack.
