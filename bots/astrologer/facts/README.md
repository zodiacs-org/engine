# Reading facts

`reading-facts.ts` computes, for one birth and a transit window, every number the Astrologer bot may state, so that the bot interprets and never calculates. It uses the engine's own code from `src/`: `resolveLocalToUtc` for civil time, `natalChart` for positions and whole sign houses, `sectOf` for sect, `findConfiguredAspects` for aspects and station contacts, `searchLongitudeCrossings` for exact transit dates, `longitudeSpeed` for stations, and `moonSignCandidates` for the Moon's signs over a day. It lives outside `src/`, so nothing here reaches `dist/` or the npm package.

What it returns: the native's calendar age at the window start (years and months from the local birth date, and a stage: infant under 2, child 2 to 12, adolescent 13 to 17, adult), and for each chart version:

- Placements: sign, degree truncated to the arcminute, whole sign house, retrograde.
- Sect from the engine (`sectOf`), not from the Sun's house, and the sect team after Ptolemy, *Tetrabiblos* I.7: by day the Sun is the sect light, Jupiter the benefic and Saturn the malefic of sect, Venus the benefic and Mars the malefic contrary to sect; by night the Moon, Venus and Mars, with Jupiter and Saturn contrary to sect. Mercury is a morning star (day sect) when it is west of the Sun in longitude, an evening star (night sect) when east. Without a birth time both teams are listed for reference and none is assigned.
- Essential dignities of the seven classical planets from the engine (`essentialDignities` and `dignityRulersAt` in `src/techniques/dignities.ts`): domicile and exaltation (*Tetrabiblos* I.17 and I.19), detriment and fall, Dorothean triplicity counting the lord of the chart's sect, Egyptian term, Chaldean face, peregrine, and the lord of each dignity at the planet's degree. Without a birth time the triplicity is given for either sect.
- The next birthday: the next calendar anniversary of the local birth date on or after the window start (1 March in a common year for a 29 February birth), and the age reached then.
- Natal aspects between the Sun to Pluto, the true node, the Ascendant and the Midheaven, with the engine's default orbs, ranked by orb, tightest first.
- The chart ruler: the traditional domicile lord of the rising sign (`dignityRulersAt`), with its placement, its dignity line, its sect role from the sect team and Mercury's phase, and its aspects from the ranking.
- Dispositors: for each body Sun to Pluto, the domicile lord of its sign (`dignityRulersAt`, traditional rulers) and the chain of lords from it, which ends at a planet in its own sign, at a loop of planets that dispose one another, or (without a birth time) at the Moon, whose sign is unknown. The final dispositor is given when every classical planet's chain ends at the same planet in its own sign; otherwise the line names where the chains end. Mutual receptions by domicile come from the engine's `mutualReceptions`.
- Planets on an angle: each conjunction or opposition of a planet or the node with the Ascendant or Midheaven in the ranking, read as on the Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven or IC, or a line saying none is. Also the points in the angular whole sign houses (1, 4, 7, 10), labelled as house placements.
- The solar return for the next birthday: the local date of the Sun's crossing of its natal longitude (`searchLongitudeCrossings`, searched within 4 days of the anniversary), marked inside or after the window. When it is inside the window it is the same date as the transit line of the Sun conjunct the natal Sun.
- The rising sign's birth-time margin (with a birth time): the minutes earlier and later, searched minute by minute up to 12 hours, after which the Ascendant is in the sign before or after, as the first [E] line.
- Points within 2 degrees of a sign edge, with the neighbouring sign and, for the Ascendant, Midheaven and Moon, the birth-time shift in minutes that moves the point across the edge.
- Transits in the window (Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto; conjunction, square, trine, opposition; to the natal Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Ascendant and Midheaven), dated in the person's zone. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, and any hit where the body moves under 0.01 degrees a day, are given as the date range within 1 degree. Each line also gives the transiting body's sign and whole sign house at its exact hits and at the ends of its range, with the dates when they differ.
- Stations in the window (Mercury to Pluto), with the station's sign and whole sign house, and, for each, the natal points within 2 degrees of a major aspect, or a statement that none is.

Every fact line of the text sheet starts with an ID in square brackets, numbered in sheet order and stable for the same input and options: N age and next birthday, P placements, K sect, D dignities, A natal aspects, R chart ruler, L dispositors, C planets on an angle, H angular houses, E sign edges, B solar return, T transits, S stations, and S1a, S1b, ... the natal contacts of station S1. The bot names a contact only by quoting its line with the ID.

A fold or gap sheet has two charts, A and B (A is the earlier instant). Each chart's lines carry its letter and are numbered within the chart (`[A-T3]`, `[B-T3]`). Before the charts, `[G1]` and `[G2]` say which clock reading gives each chart (the offset it is read on, before or after the change, the UTC instant and what the zone's clocks showed). After them, the `[X]` lines compare the two charts line by line: rising sign and chart ruler, sect, the planets whose sign or house differs, planets on an angle, the tightest aspect, natal aspects in one chart only, transits with the same dates in both (as ID pairs), transits with different dates and transits in one chart only, and the same for station contacts and their orbs.

A repeated local time (`fold`) gives both charts, a skipped one (`gap`) both candidates, and an unknown time a reference chart at local noon with planets only: no houses, angles, chart ruler, angle contacts, sect or Moon degree, and the Moon's signs over the local date.

Run it from the repository root after `npm ci`:

```sh
# one birth, text sheet (add --json for the structured facts)
node_modules/.bin/vite-node bots/astrologer/facts/cli.ts -- \
  '{"date":"1998-04-04","time":"14:44","timeKnown":true,"zone":"Europe/Istanbul","lat":41.0082,"lon":28.9784}'

# many births: a JSON object of id to input on stdin, a JSON object of id to sheet on stdout
node_modules/.bin/vite-node bots/astrologer/facts/cli.ts -- --batch < inputs.json > sheets.json

# options: --window-start YYYY-MM-DD (default 2026-11-01), --days N (default 90)
```

## Checking a reading

`check-reading.ts` reads a reading against its sheet. It finds every aspect, transit or station contact the reading names (two points and an aspect word close together in a clause, in either order, including "on", "meets", "returns" and aspects to the Descendant or IC) and flags it when no sheet line has the same two points with the same aspect, or, for a transit or station, the same direction (moving body first, natal point second). It also flags an ID that is not on the sheet, and an ID quoted with another line's planets.

Angle claims are read as conjunctions with the angle: "on the Ascendant", "the rising degree", "conjunct the MC", "Saturn rising", "Jupiter culminating", and "on the Ascendant axis" (which passes with a conjunction or an opposition). A rising sign ("Scorpio rising", "the rising sign") is not a claim. A violation of an angle claim quotes the sheet's lines of planets on an angle.

Birthday claims are checked against the next birthday and solar return lines: a date written next to "birthday" or "solar return" must be one of those dates, "the day after (or N days before) the birthday" must agree with the date it is said of (in the same sentence or the one before), and "turns N" or "the Nth birthday" must give the sheet's age. Dates are read as ISO dates, "11 November (2026)" or "November 11(, 2026)".

Dates, ranges and orbs are checked against the line they are tied to. A sentence is tied to the IDs it quotes, the sheet relations it names, the station lines of planets it says station, and every transit or station line of any other point it names. Each date in it must be a date of those lines (with the year, when the reading gives one), the window's ends or the birth date; a sentence that names no point may give any date on the sheet. A span ("from D1 to D2", "D1 to D2", "between D1 and D2", "20 to 22 November") must be one transit line's in-orb range or the window, so two lines' dates joined into a span are flagged. "Through the window", "until the end of the window" and "from the start of the window" must be covered by the tied line's range. An orb must be written as on a tied line.

Houses and signs: "Saturn sits in your tenth house", "your third-house Venus", "transiting Saturn in Aries". A house or sign said of a planet that is not moving in the sentence must be its natal one (from the placement lines). For a transiting or stationing planet (named on a quoted transit or station line, marked "transiting", or said to station, also in the next sentence) it must be the one its line gives; a moving planet not marked "transiting" may also be given its natal house. A house or sign with no planet of its own in a sentence about a transit or station ("It tests ... in your tenth house", with the sentence before when the sentence names no point) must be the moving body's from its line or the natal house or sign of a point the sentence or its lines name. A planet named with no ID and not marked "transiting" is natal, except in a dated sentence, after "now" or "then", or under a heading of the 90 days (a month, "the 90 days", "calendar"), where its transit or station line's sign or house also passes. Its claim is found through an appositive ("Venus, the benefic of sect, is in Virgo"), a parenthesis, or as the clause's only planet ("Mars rules the chart and sits in Taurus in the seventh house"). Claims in a hypothetical ("would be in Pisces if the birth time were later") are not read. Older sheets give no sign or house for transiting bodies, so any such claim is flagged.

The sheet marks a natal aspect or angle contact "out of sign" when its two points are not the aspect's number of signs apart (a conjunction in two signs, a square three signs apart and so on), and gives the number of planets on an angle in the [C] heading. A count in the reading ("three planets sit on angles", "no planet is on an angle") must be that number, with or without the node, in the chart it is said of.

Angle paraphrases are read as the angle: "the rising edge", "the eastern horizon" (Ascendant), "the setting edge" (Descendant), "at the top of the chart" (Midheaven), "at the bottom of the chart" (IC), "on the horizon" and "on the meridian" (the axis), "Venus setting" (on the Descendant), and every planet in "the Moon and Venus on the Ascendant". "Angular" said of a planet passes only when a [C] line puts it on an angle or the [H] line in an angular house.

Rulers: "Mercury rules your second house", "the ruler of the Leo second, the Sun", "your tenth-house ruler, Saturn", "the second house, ruled by Mars" must name the traditional ruler of the house's sign in whole sign houses from the rising sign (and "the Leo second" must be the house's sign); "Mercury rules Leo" the sign's ruler; "Venus rules the chart" or "your ruler, Venus" the [R1] chart ruler; "its ruler, Jupiter" and "Uranus is ruled by Jupiter" the domicile lord of the planet's sign. Modern co-rulers (Pluto, Uranus, Neptune) pass only in a sentence that says "modern". Negated and hypothetical claims are not read.

Angle contacts and houses: a sentence that names the Midheaven or the IC and calls it the tenth or fourth house ("[A11] Jupiter square Midheaven touches ... (the tenth house)") is flagged when the sheet puts that angle in another whole sign house, unless another point the sentence names, or a line it quotes, is in that house, or the house is negated ("not the tenth house").

Also: a house or sign said of a group ("[A1] Moon trine Mercury and [A13] Sun conjunction Mercury, both in the eighth house", "the Sun, Moon and Mercury are all in Scorpio") must be the natal one of every planet named before it in the clause; "near the window's end (start)" needs the dates the sentence gives or quotes in the window's last (first) third, and "late (early) in the window" in its second (first) half; a line said to fall "the same day" or "that day" must share a date with the dates or lines before it in the sentence, or else in the sentence before; a list of planets on an angle called complete ("these are the only angle placements", "no other planet is on an angle") must name, in its paragraph, every [C] line of each chart it covers; and a birth-time shift said to keep the rising sign ("141 minutes later ... but not the rising sign") must be shorter than the rising sign line's margin that way.

Dispositor chains: summary claims are checked against the [L] lines. "Every chain ends at X", "everything runs back to X" and "X at the end of every chain" need every chain to end at X (or at a loop with X in it); "most chains lead to X" (also "nearly every", "the majority") needs more than half the chains, or more than end at any other place; "the chains end at X and Y" needs each to be a place a chain ends; "Mars's chain ends at Venus", "your Sun and Moon chains lead to Mars" and "the Sun, Moon and Venus all lead back to it" need each named chain to end there; and "X is the final dispositor" must match [L11]. "It", "this pair" and "their loop" are read as the planets named before them in the sentence, or in the last sentence that names one. Negated and hypothetical claims are not read.

Two charts (fold or gap): each claim is checked against the chart it is attributed to, by a chart named in the sentence ("chart B", "the earlier occurrence", "the later", "in B"), a chart letter in a quoted ID, a heading or bold lead naming one chart, or an earlier sentence of the same paragraph that names one. A contact said to be in both charts must be in both; one said to be only in one chart ("appear only in the earlier chart", also "the Sun contacts to the Ascendant") must be missing from the other; a contact only one chart has, named with no chart, is flagged; a natal house or sign that holds in one chart only must name it; and a date or orb of one chart's line, given with no chart, must also be on the other chart's matching line. A sentence that names both charts or quotes both charts' lines is read as a comparison and only its "only in" claims are checked. The clock state tied to a chart ("a clock that had not yet changed (so chart A)") must be the side of the change the sheet reads that chart's time on, and a birth "before the clocks moved" is chart A, the earlier instant.

It is a heuristic over English: it misses relations whose subject is far from the aspect word, and it reads a chart only from the words above.

```sh
node_modules/.bin/vite-node bots/astrologer/facts/check-cli.ts -- --sheet sheet.txt --reading reading.md [--json]
# exit 0: no violation; 1: violations (printed); 2: bad arguments
```

The grading harness (`grading/run.py`) runs it after the writer. When it fails, the writer gets the flagged sentences with their problems and returns a replacement for each, which is spliced into the reading in place; the whole reading is rewritten only when no flagged sentence can be located or the reply cannot be read. At most 2 revisions; each chart's checks, with the revision mode, are recorded in `checks/<id>.json`.

Tests: `npx vitest run bots/astrologer/facts`. Typecheck: `npx tsc -p bots/astrologer/facts/tsconfig.json`. The tests pin structure and the engine's own consistency (ranking order, station speed sign, contacts against a separately written separation); they are regression tests, not accuracy evidence.
